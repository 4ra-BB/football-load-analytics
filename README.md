# Football Load & Performance Analytics

An analytics tool for football coaching staff, built to turn daily
player-reported data into decisions about training load, injury risk and match
preparation.

**[Live demo →](https://your-app.streamlit.app)** *(interface in Spanish)*

---

## What problem this solves

Most amateur and semi-professional clubs collect wellness questionnaires and
never look at them again. The data sits in a spreadsheet, nobody aggregates it,
and by the time a player gets injured the warning signs have been buried in
three hundred rows.

This tool reads those daily responses and surfaces what matters: who is
accumulating load, who has been reporting pain for several days in a row, and
whether the pattern of goals conceded points to a physical or a tactical problem.

It was originally built for a real club and is in active use. The public version
runs on synthetic data.

---

## What it does

**Internal load monitoring**
- Hooper Index tracked per player and grouped by position, with the coaching
  staff's own alert thresholds shaded on the chart
- 30-day averages of the five wellness items, so a rising index can be traced
  back to sleep, fatigue, muscle soreness or stress
- Reported discomfort over the last seven days, including how many consecutive
  days each player has flagged the same complaint
- Session RPE by player and position, with a count of high-intensity sessions

**Match performance**
- Minutes, starts, goals, assists and cards per player
- Goals for and against by 15-minute block, which separates "we concede early in
  the second half" from "we fade in the last twenty minutes"

**Opposition analysis**
- Top scorers of the upcoming opponent, with the share of team goals each one
  accounts for
- The opponent's own scoring and conceding pattern by match phase

**Individual player view**
- Hooper and RPE evolution against the team average, with reported-discomfort
  days marked on the chart
- Accumulated indicators combining internal load and match participation

**Reports**
- Two separate PDFs: one for load, which contains health data, and one for match
  preparation. The split is deliberate - see *Data protection* below.

## Methodology

**Hooper Index** — the sum of sleep quality, fatigue, muscle soreness and stress,
each on a 1–5 scale where 1 is the best possible state and 5 the worst. Range
4–20; higher means worse. Traffic light: 4–8 good, 9–13 monitor, 14–20 alert.

All four items point in the same direction, so they are summed directly without
inversion. This is the most common error when reproducing the index from
positively-worded questionnaires.

**RPE** — Borg CR10 scale (0 = complete rest, 10 = maximal effort). The raw
perceived value is used, not multiplied by session duration. Session load
(RPE × minutes) and acute:chronic workload ratio are not implemented, because the
source questionnaire does not capture session duration. This is a known
limitation, not an oversight.

**Match phases** — 0-14, 15-29, 30-end of first half, 45-59, 60-74, 75-end.
Stoppage-time goals are recorded as minute 45 and 90 respectively.

**Missing data is never interpolated.** A gap in a player's line means they did
not answer that day. Questionnaire adherence is information in itself, and
smoothing over it would hide a real problem. For the same reason, streak counts
of reported discomfort are shown alongside the number of days actually answered:
a four-day streak with two missing responses is weaker evidence than a four-day
streak with four.

## The data

All data in this repository is synthetic. No real person appears in it.

- **Teams and players** — a fictional ten-team league. Player names are
  deliberately gender-neutral and were chosen to avoid resemblance to real
  footballers.
- **Fixtures** — a double round-robin generated by the circle method: 18
  matchdays, 5 matches each, 9 home and 9 away per team.
- **Scorelines and goal minutes** — taken from the 2025 Chilean Primera División
  and reassigned to the fictional fixture list. Match phases therefore reflect a
  realistic distribution of when goals actually happen.
- **Lineups, wellness and RPE** — generated with a seeded simulation. Player
  baselines differ, questionnaire adherence is imperfect, and goalscorers are
  constrained to players who were actually on the pitch at that minute.

Three situations were built into the data on purpose, so that the tool has
something to detect:

1. A midfielder with the highest minute load in the squad shows rising RPE and
   muscle soreness, reports hamstring tension six days before going off injured,
   and misses the last two matchdays.
2. A defender's mood and stress scores deteriorate over two weeks, coinciding
   with a yellow card, a red card and a one-match suspension.
3. The goalkeeping position alternates between two players for the first half of
   the season before settling on one.

The schema and the seed data live in `sql/`. Everything was modelled and
populated in PostgreSQL (Supabase) and exported to CSV; the app reads the CSVs so
that the public demo needs no credentials.

## Running locally

```bash
git clone https://github.com/yourname/football-load-analytics.git
cd football-load-analytics
pip install -r requirements.txt
streamlit run app.py
```

Built with Python, pandas, Matplotlib and Streamlit. Data modelled in PostgreSQL.

---

## Limitations

- Session duration is not collected, so session load and acute:chronic workload
  ratio cannot be computed. This is the single most valuable addition the tool
  could receive.
- Alert thresholds are absolute. Deviation from each player's own baseline would
  be more informative, but needs several weeks of data to be stable.
- Opposition scorers are identified by shirt number only, since there is no squad
  list to validate names against.
